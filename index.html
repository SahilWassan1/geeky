Golden dataset and the auto-publish gate

200 historical amendments with known correct output, scored per changeType. A class is allowed to publish unattended only while it measures above 99% on this set. Two classes are below the gate today, and one is blocked whatever it scores.

Why commencement is hard-blocked

Commencement scope in Indian law cannot be determined mechanically. Sections of an act are routinely brought into force in tranches years apart, and a notification appointing a date rarely enumerates which sub-provisions it actually switches on. A model reading that text produces a confident answer to a question the text does not settle. So the rule is structural, not statistical: commencement never auto-promotes past auto, and the score above is reported only to keep us honest about how wrong it would be.

How a notification becomes a record

Three tiers, one pipeline

auto — machine-extracted, live immediately, labelled as unchecked. reconciled — two independent sources produced an identical record. verified — a human cleared it. Speed comes from tier one, trust from tier three, and the label on the record is what keeps that honest. Nothing is ever silently upgraded: every promotion is written into the record's own audit trail, visible from the drawer on any entry.

The mechanical check

For a substitution, the extracted old text must appear verbatim exactly once at the cited section of the base text. Exactly once matters as much as verbatim: if the quoted text occurs twice, the amendment could attach to either position, and the change is not provable. Pass means the change is mechanically provable and can publish unattended. Fail routes to review — and still publishes at auto, because a labelled unchecked record is more useful than a missing one.

Sourcing

The corpus is built from primary sources only — India Code for the baseline, the Gazette of India for authority, ministry notification pages for speed. Never from a competitor's compilation: those are protectable by selection and arrangement. Every source respects robots.txt and a rate limit, and every row carries its provenance.

What is not built yet

  1. Storage with version history. Records live in a flat file. They need a database that can render any section as it stood on any date.
  2. The full source registry. Nine of the forty-two sources are configured with a parser and a dead-man's-switch threshold.
  3. Two classes below the gate. Supersession under-reports which allied rule sets it sweeps up; that is an extraction problem with a known fix.
  4. Human queue tooling. Reviewers currently clear records against the PDF by hand. The diff view here is the seed of the tool they need.
